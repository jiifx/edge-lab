Part 1 — Getting started

1. What Edge Lab does

Edge Lab answers two questions a prop trader has to get right:

  • Is my edge real? Or is my record still explainable by luck?
  • How big should I trade? What size passes this firm's evaluation, keeps the funded account alive, and pays me?

It has two modes, switched with the buttons at the top right:

ModeUse it to
JournalLog your trades, review them, and see what your record says: stats, strengths and leaks, timing, calendar, and how close each account is to its firm's rules.
SimulatorRun your edge thousands of times against a firm's rules: whether the edge is real, your odds of passing, your payouts once funded, and whether the challenge is worth buying.

The two modes connect through one switch, Use my journal. When it is on, the Simulator runs on your logged trades instead of the sliders.

2. The one unit: R

1R is the amount you planned to lose on a trade, the distance from entry to stop times your size. A trade that makes twice what you risked is +2R. A full stop-out is −1R. A trade closed at break-even is a scratch (0R).

Edge Lab works in R because R does not depend on account size. Your edge is the same whether you trade a $25,000 or a $150,000 account. Dollars come in at the end: set what 1R is worth (Journal) or your risk per trade (Simulator), and the app converts.

Why it matters

A trade with no stop, no Risk $ and no R override has no R. It still counts toward win rate and dollar totals, but every R statistic leaves it out. Log a stop or a Risk $ on every trade.

4. Quick start

  1. Create an account. In Journal, click + New in Account scope. Name it after the prop account, e.g. "Apex 50k".
  2. Log your trades. Click + New trade (Ctrl+N). At minimum: instrument, session, and a result (prices, or Risk $ plus P&L, or an R value).
  3. Set the firm. Switch to Simulator, click Edit rules in The firm, pick a preset and match every field to your firm.
  4. Turn on Use my journal. It needs at least 10 resolved trades. The Simulator now runs on your record.
  5. Read the four tabs in order: Validate (is it real?) → Challenge (will it pass?) → Funded (will it pay?) → Decision (is it worth the fee?).
  6. Bind the firm to the account. Back in Journal, pick the account in Account scope and click Bind sim firm in Rule guard. The rule meters now track that account live.

Part 2 — Journal

5. Accounts

Every trade belongs to one account. Keep one journal account per prop account. The rule guard and prop odds treat an account's trades as the real history of that account.

  • Create: + New, type a name, confirm.
  • Switch: pick it in the Account scope dropdown. The log, stats, filters, equity and odds all follow the scope.
  • All accounts: combines everything. Useful for overall stats. Rule guard needs a single account.
  • Delete: select the account, click Delete, confirm. Its trades and screenshots are removed permanently. Export a backup first.

Common mix-up

7. The trade log

  • Trades are grouped by day with a daily total. Click a trade to open its detail view.
  • Detail view: ← / → steps through trades. Edit opens the editor. Click a screenshot to zoom: scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to fit.
  • Unit switch (top right): show results in R, P&L or Both. P&L needs dollars turned on (section 9).
  • Bulk delete: Select, tick trades or use All, then Delete (n).

8. Filters

Filters narrow the log and every Stats tab. Combine any of: text search (notes and plan), setup, entry model, outcome, direction, session, mistake tag, market condition, instrument, date range. Reset clears them all.

Clicking a row in the Edge report adds a segment filter chip above the log. Click its × to remove it.

9. Equity and dollars

The journal works in R by default. To see money:

  1. Pick a single account in Account scope.
  2. Click Show dollars in the Equity panel.
  3. Enter a Starting balance.
  4. Enter What 1R is worth, either $ per R (fixed dollars) or % of start.

Trades logged in R only are then priced at that value. Trades with a logged P&L keep their own figure. The line under the inputs says how many trades are priced.

On All accounts, combined dollars only work when every account has a balance and the same R value.

10. Rule guard

Rule guard shows how far this account is from breaking its firm's rules: drawdown headroom, today's loss against the daily limit, distance to target, min days and consistency.

  1. Set the firm's rules in the Simulator (section 18).
  2. In Journal, pick one account in Account scope.
  3. Click Bind sim firm. The firm is now stored with this account. Rebind sim firm replaces it. Unbind removes it.
  4. Set the phase: eval or funded. Funded replaces the target meter with payout readiness and starts counting from the funding date.

What it assumes

That this journal account is the prop account: its trades are the trades placed there, at those sizes. On a general journal covering everything you trade, the meters describe an account that does not exist. Add a separate account per prop account.

Meters are computed from logged trade closes, not tick data. Keep a buffer on trailing rules.

The day guard line shows today's trades and R. It warns when you have 2+ losses in a row, 3+ losses today, or an elevated logged emotion. Stop for the day when you see it.

11. Stats — Performance

How to read it

  • The four headline cards are the edge: Expectancy, Win rate, Reward : risk, Profit factor. Expectancy is the one that grows the account. Win rate and reward:risk only mean something together.
  • Bands (95% CI) under a number show how sure the sample is. If the expectancy band touches 0, luck is not ruled out yet.
  • The tile grid adds trades, totals, balance, SQN, volatility, deepest drawdown, streaks, Kelly, recent form, best-day share and tail ratio. Each is defined in the glossary. These unlock at 8+ resolved trades.
  • Best-day share matters for consistency rules. Many firms cap one day's share of profit near 30–50%.

Prop odds

Your journaled trades, resampled against the firm (the bound firm, or the Simulator's firm if none is bound), at the Decision tab's eval and funded risk. It shows pass odds, attempts and fees to fund, the chance to stay funded a year, the chance a purchase ends in a payout, expected payout, and sample quality. It needs 10 resolved trades. Under 50 trades, treat every range as directional.

Charts

  • Equity curve in R, oldest to newest. R-multiple distribution: red losses, green wins.
  • Rolling expectancy over a 30-trade window. A falling line means the edge is drifting or the market changed.

Exit management (needs MFE/MAE on 30+ trades)

  • Exit efficiency = realised R ÷ best available R (MFE).
  • Avg MFE is the average best point a trade reached. Compare it with your average winner: the gap is what your exits cost.
  • Avg heat (MAE) is how far trades went against you on average. Full-risk touches near 100% mean stops fire late or get moved.
  • How far trades run — by session: the band is the middle half of runs, the dark mark the median, the light mark the best 10%. Kept is the share of the run you realised.

Target sweep — what should the R:R be?

Your stop is 1R, so a target of T pays +T if the move reached T before coming back to the stop, and −1R if it did not. The sweep applies that to your record at every target and shows expectancy per trade.

  1. Give every trade a Planned R:R (typed, or from entry/stop/target). If you traded one target throughout, type it in the Planned R:R across this account box and click Set on n with none. Replace on all overwrites every trade.
  2. Log Full run (R) on your winners, at least 20 of them.
  3. Read the flat top, not the single peak. Every target inside the flat top pays about the same; the lower end fills more often and pays sooner. The shaded range shows where 90% of resamples of your record put the best target.

Don't set the target to your average MFE

The average sits above most trades. Every winner that peaks short of a higher target turns into a full −1R loss. Going from target P to T only pays if p(T)·(T+1) > p(P)·(P+1). At a 48% win rate, moving 1.5R → 3R needs 63% of your current winners to also reach 3R.

The sweep starts at your lowest planned target: a trade that ran halfway and reversed is recorded only as a loss, so nothing below it can be measured. It assumes the stop stays put and that you would really hold to the new target. It follows the log filter, so filter to one setup or session to get a target for that subset. If runs are logged on under 80% of winners it warns you: logging runs mostly on big days biases it toward bigger targets.

12. Stats — Edge report

  • Discipline check: clean trades against trades with a tagged mistake.
  • Strengths — lean in / Leaks — cut these: every tag, grade, emotion, setup, session and direction with 4+ trades, ranked. Click a row to filter the log to those trades.
  • Direction: long against short. Total R, expectancy and win rate side by side. The toggle picks which side's win/loss breakdown is shown. Under 8 trades a side, it's a hint, not a finding.
  • By entry model / setup / quality tag / session: n, win%, RR (average winner ÷ average loser), expectancy, total R. Click an entry model row to filter the log. A row under 4 trades is noise.
  • By session radar: where your R comes from. The dashed ring is 0R. Untagged trades count as Other. Open a trade and set its session to move it.

Read it as hypotheses

With ~20 segments on screen, one will look best by chance. A leak is worth acting on when it has a real sample behind it and still holds after you log more trades.

13. Stats — Timing

The same record cut by clock: entry hour, weekday, holding time, which trade of the day, how long you waited after a loss, and how busy the day was.

"Does any of it hold up?"

The box at the top tests whether any one bucket really differs from the rest. It needs 30+ trades on an axis in 2+ buckets of 4+. It reports the most striking bucket, its gap against the rest, and the noise floor: the gap that picking the best of that many buckets produces on a record with no timing edge at all. Stands out means the gap cleared both the 90% band and the noise floor. Nothing stands out yet means it didn't. Log more trades in the thin buckets rather than changing your trading.

Even when a bucket stands out, an hour is when you traded, not what. Check the setup, session and direction mix inside it on the Edge report before changing anything.

15. Backups and your data

  • Desktop app: the journal is journal.db (SQLite) plus screenshots in images\, inside Documents\PropEdgeLab\. Open folder in the rail reveals it.
  • Browser version: trades live in that browser only. Export backups often.

Part 3 — Simulator

Every Simulator number comes from Monte Carlo: your edge is played trade by trade, thousands of times, against the firm's rules. Internally everything is in R, scaled by your risk per trade, then shown in dollars.

Use my journal

  1. Turn on Use my journal. It needs 10+ resolved trades with an R basis.
  2. Pick the Edge source: one account, or all.

Every tab now resamples your real trades instead of the sliders. Trades without a stop or Risk $ are left out; the line under the switch says how many.

Several accounts, one signal

If you fire the same trades into several accounts, pooling them counts one decision two or three times and makes every range look more certain than it is. Set Edge source to a single account. Validate flags pooled and mirrored trades.

17. Leave out (what if I stop…)

With Use my journal on, Leave out removes one segment of your record (the same segments as the Edge report, e.g. "Direction · Short"). Every tab then recomputes without it. The box below shows:

  • the change in expectancy with its 90% band;
  • the noise floor: how much cutting the most striking of that many segments moves a record when nothing is going on;
  • a verdict: Stands out, Inside the noise floor, Band includes zero or Barely moves the record;
  • a warning if survival drops. Fewer trades is a shorter record, not automatically a safer one.

Even "Stands out" is a hypothesis: you picked the segment by looking at these same trades. Trade it forward before believing it. Each segment you test is another look at the same record, so Validate widens its ranges for every look (see "Multiple looks" in section 23). Clearing the cut does not undo that. If the cut leaves fewer than 30 trades, it is not applied.

19. Validate — is your edge real?

Steps

  1. Set your edge (sliders or journal).
  2. Read the verdict: Likely real, Promising, unproven, Not enough data or No edge.
  3. Set the drawdown Horizon (how many trades ahead you plan for) and, optionally, your own Account drawdown limit % (0 = the firm's).
  4. Read the largest risk per trade box at the bottom.

Reading the verdict

  • Likely real: the 95% range for expectancy stays above zero.
  • Promising, unproven: the average is positive, but the range still crosses zero. More trades will settle it.
  • Not enough data: under 30 trades. Aim for 100+. Everything below still prints; only the verdict holds back.

Each figure (win rate, expectancy, profit factor) has its range underneath. Sample shows the trade count and how many independent observations they amount to. Trades that cluster by day, setup or account are not separate draws, so every range is computed from the effective count.

The curve is where your true average trade could sit. The shaded band is the 95% range, with its edges marked by dashed lines in the band's colour; the red dashed line is break-even. If the band crosses break-even, you cannot yet rule out having no edge.

Drawdown to plan for

Maximum drawdown grows with how long you trade (roughly with log of trades), so every number names its horizon and percentile. Your record is resampled first, then the future simulated from each resample, so the answer covers the edge you might actually have, not just the one you measured.

p50 — coin flipThe median. Not a plan.
p75 — 1 in 4 worseOne run in four is deeper.
p90 — size to thisThe sizing anchor.
p99 — worst caseThe bad year. Size to survive it.

On the chart: solid line p90, dashed p99, dotted p50; the vertical line is your horizon. Every point is the same edge. The curve rises because a longer run has more chances to go wrong.

  • Resampling — Block (fitted) keeps runs of consecutive trades together, so clustered losses survive the resampling. The block length is fitted from your record's lag-1 autocorrelation. Independent draws each trade separately and understates drawdown when losses cluster. The line under it shows the fitted block and, if clustered, what independent draws would have said.
  • Use realised loss sizes: on = losses are resampled at the sizes you actually logged, so a stop that filled at −1.4R can happen again. Off = every loss is your average loss. If all your logged losses are the same size, your journal records planned losses, not fills.

Largest risk per trade

The risk per trade at which the p95 drawdown over your horizon exactly fills the account limit. It is shown in green if the Funded tab's risk is inside it, red if not, with how much of the limit a 1-in-20 bad run would use. One run in twenty is still worse, so treat it as a ceiling, not a target.

Warnings on this tab

  • Multiple looks: you have tested several subsets with Leave out, so ranges are widened (each is a stricter interval, so the whole family still carries 95%).
  • Pooled accounts: the edge pools several accounts; mirrored trades (same instrument and direction within 5 minutes in different accounts) are discounted.
  • The full record, for comparison: shown whenever a Leave-out cut is active.

20. Challenge — will it pass?

  1. Set Risk per trade % for the evaluation.
  2. Read chance to pass, its 90% range, and typical trading days to pass.
  3. Read the one-line advice under it (see below).
  4. Use ↻ re-roll to draw a new sample of paths. On a 2-step firm, Phase 1 / Phase 2 switches which phase is drawn.
  • Paths chart: each line is a live trade-by-trade walk of your edge against the rules. The pass figure itself averages 2,600 attempts.
  • How attempts end: pass, drawdown, daily limit, timeout. On a 2-step firm the faded end of a bar is failure in phase 2.
  • Days to pass: trading days, not calendar days; the total across both phases on a 2-step. The time limit applies per phase.
  • Pass phase 1 × then pass phase 2 multiply into the headline on a 2-step firm.

  3. Read the results and the chart.

The bracket marks the flat top: sizes that pay within 3% of the best, but that do not survive the same. The solid line is your size; a dashed line marks the smallest size for your goal.

The withdrawal habit this tab assumes

Up to 5% of the account is swept every 5 trading days, never out of the drawdown cushion. On a static or locked-trailing floor, a sweep only happens if the account stays a full max-drawdown above its floor afterwards: on $50,000 with a $2,000 drawdown, $2,000 stays in and the first withdrawal needs about $4,500 of profit. That retained cushion is most of what "Survival" measures. Keeping $500 less costs about 16 points of survival; $1,000 less costs about 51. If you sweep down to the firm's minimum, the survival figure does not describe you. On an unlocked trailing floor nothing is retained, because the floor follows the withdrawal down.

Payout gates (buffer, % cap, $ cap, consistency, winning days) are applied exactly as the firm would. Payout cadence is shown but not priced. Trades are drawn independently: no tilt, no streaks, no sizing up after a loss.

22. Decision — is it worth buying?

  1. Set Eval risk / trade % and Funded risk / trade %. These two also drive the journal's prop odds.
  2. Read Net expected value of buying in and its verdict: Worth it, Not worth it, or Could go either way (graded on the low end of the 90% range).
  3. If it's not worth it, read the line under the verdict: it names an eval size that would make it pay, or says no size will.
Chance to passEnd-to-end pass odds (both phases on a 2-step).
Expected fees to fundAverage cost to get funded, re-attempting until you pass: fees, resets, subscription months, activation.
Best eval sizeThe eval size that funds you for the least money, measured on this firm; where several cost the same, the quickest. Under a time limit or min days, a smaller size can cost more.
First-year payoutExpected withdrawals over a funded year, after split.
Pass → paidChance one purchase ends with money withdrawn; in brackets, the odds once funded and typical days to the first payout.
Net @ 1st payoutWhere you stand the day of your first withdrawal: the odds of getting there × one payout, minus the cost to fund. A firm can be worth a year and still not have repaid its fee by then.

Budget to funded shows what to set aside to be 50%, 80% or 95% sure of getting funded, with attempts and months. Plan from these rather than the average when evaluations come out of savings. They assume attempts are independent; real traders tilt after failures, so treat them as floors.

The net EV assumes your edge is real (check Validate), that you re-attempt until funded, and about a year of trading. It ignores time value and taxes. Discounted resets are priced only when a Reset fee is set.

24. What the model assumes

  • Your record is a fair sample of your future trading. A record can't check this about itself.
  • Trades are drawn independently in Challenge, Funded and Decision: no tilt, no streaks, no sizing up after a loss. (Validate's drawdown uses block resampling to keep clusters.)
  • The firm's published rules hold as entered. Min days and consistency are modelled approximately.
  • Withdrawal habit: up to 5% of the account every 5 trading days, never out of the drawdown cushion (section 21).
  • Not modelled: payout cadence after the first payout, the risk of a firm not paying (collapse, rule changes, denied withdrawals), time value of money, taxes.
  • Why ranges, not single numbers: every figure from your journal is re-run on resamples of your own record, so it shows the edge you might have, not just the one you measured. A range that crosses a line (zero, 85% survival) means the record can't yet say which side you're on.

25. Glossary

RWhat you planned to lose on one trade. All results are multiples of it.
Resolved / decided / scratchResolved = finished. Decided = a win or a loss. Scratch = closed at 0R, neither.
Win rateShare of decided trades that won. On its own it decides nothing: 35% pays well if winners are 3× losers.
ExpectancyWhat one average trade is worth, in R. +0.20R = every trade adds a fifth of one risk unit on average.
Reward : riskAverage winner ÷ average loser. With win rate, it is the edge.
Profit factorGross wins ÷ gross losses. Under 1 you pay to trade; 1.25–1.5 is workable; over 3 usually means a small sample or one big winner.
Total R / Total P&LEverything added up, in R or money.
BalanceStarting balance plus every priced trade in the account, whatever the filter.
SQNSystem Quality Number (Van Tharp): expectancy ÷ its volatility, scaled by sample size; graded at n = 100.
σ per tradeSpread of R outcomes. Higher = bumpier ride and deeper holes for the same expectancy.
Deepest drawdown so farThe worst peak-to-trough fall this record has taken. One realisation, not a floor; Validate gives the forward distribution.
StreaksLongest win and loss runs. Size for the loss run.
Kelly f*The growth-maximising bet fraction, shown as the range your win-rate interval implies. Prop traders use a fraction of the lower end.
Recent formAverage R over the last 20 trades against the whole record.
Best-day shareHow much profit came from one day, measured over windows the size of one evaluation target, the way firms measure it.
Tail ratioWorst single loss ÷ average loss. Above 2 = a stop didn't hold or got moved.
MFE / MAEMaximum favourable / adverse excursion: how far a trade ran for and against you while open.
Full runHow far price got before it would have hit your stop, whether or not you were still in.
Exit efficiencyRealised R ÷ MFE.
p50 / p90 / p95 / p99Percentiles: the value that 50 / 90 / 95 / 99 runs in 100 stay within.
CI / band / rangeThe spread of plausible values given your sample.
Effective sampleHow many independent observations your trades amount to after clustering.
Noise floorHow big a gap you get for free by picking the best of many buckets on a record with no real effect.
Flat topSizes or targets that pay within 3% of the best.
Static / trailing / EODDrawdown floor types; see section 18.
Pass → paidOne purchase all the way to money out.
