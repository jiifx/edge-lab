How to use this

What R actually means

R is one unit of risk: entry to stop. If your stop costs you $100, that's 1R. A trade that made $150 made 1.5R.

Working in R instead of dollars makes every trade comparable no matter what size you took. A 1.5R win is a 1.5R win on a $5k account or a $200k one.

Fill in the three numbers at the top

  • Win rate. Count your last 40 trades, divide winners by total.
  • Reward : risk. Average what your winners paid, in R.
  • Trades this is based on. Be honest. It's the one that decides whether the other two mean anything.

Reading question one

Positive expectancy means you make money on average. The range under it is the part people skip. With few trades it's enormous, and a number sitting in a range that crosses zero isn't evidence of anything yet.

That's why the verdict changes at 30 trades. Below that you can't tell an edge from a good week. Nothing magic about the number, it's roughly where the range stops being wider than the answer.

Reading question two

The coloured bar is 1,000 attempts at the challenge, each one a simulated run of your edge against those rules. Blue passed. The reds are how it died, darkest first: total drawdown, one bad day, ran out of time.

Drag risk per trade up and watch. Profit per trade rises, red grows faster. Bigger size doesn't just win more, it burns through the attempts you had left.

Before you trust any of this

It's a calculator running your assumptions, not a measurement of your trading. It believes whatever you type. Every trade is treated as independent, identically sized, spread evenly through the day. Real trading isn't any of those. Use it to see how the pieces push against each other, not as a forecast. And it can't verify a single thing you enter, so the numbers you feed it are the whole thing. Optimistic inputs don't give you a slightly optimistic answer, they give you a confident wrong one.
